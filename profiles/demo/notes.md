# Kingfisher — working notes

A fictional boat, for the demo profile. Nothing here describes a real vessel; the point is
to give the companion something realistic to be asked about.

## The engine

A 4-cylinder diesel, freshwater cooled through a heat exchanger, raw water taken from a
seacock in the forward end of the engine bay. The thermostat opens near 82 °C and the
normal running band is 78–88 °C.

## Cooling — what to look at, in order

1. **The raw-water strainer**, forward of the engine, behind the removable panel. Weed and
   plastic bags are the usual finding. Shut the seacock before opening it, and remember to
   open it again — more engines are cooked by a forgotten seacock than by a failed pump.
2. **The impeller**, behind the bronze cover on the front of the pump. Six screws. Count the
   vanes when it comes out: a missing one is downstream, and it must be found.
3. **The heat exchanger tube stack** — if the raw side is clear and it still runs warm.

## Winter lay-up

Antifoul, anodes, impeller out for the winter, fuel tank full to keep condensation out, and
the seacocks shut and labelled.

## The autopilot

A below-decks unit driving the quadrant, talking NMEA 2000. It holds a compass heading well
and wanders in a following sea. The standby button on the pedestal is the one that matters:
it is reachable from the wheel without looking.
